The work

What the Bee Squad does

Beekeeping is one of the ways we practice agritherapy at the farm. Working with bees teaches patience, focus, and calm, and gives veterans a peaceful, purposeful job outdoors.

Caring for the hives

Squad members raise and care for the farm's honeybees: building and cleaning hives, bringing in wild swarms, splitting colonies, collecting honey, and keeping the hives healthy.

No experience is needed. You learn as you go, alongside our Lead Beekeeper.

Roots

A short history of beekeeping for veterans

Beekeeping as a way to heal after service is more than a century old.

1919 A government pamphlet recommends beekeeping for World War I veterans home with shell shock, the term then used for what we now call PTSD.
2015 Heroes to Hives begins, and grows into a nine-month beekeeping course for veterans through Michigan State University Extension.
2018 Bees4Vets starts in Reno, Nevada, inspired by that 1919 pamphlet, and works with university researchers to study whether beekeeping helps.

Most of what we know so far comes from veterans themselves, who describe a sense of purpose, calm, and focus. Formal research is still catching up. Our Bee Squad carries on the same quiet, hands-on work.
